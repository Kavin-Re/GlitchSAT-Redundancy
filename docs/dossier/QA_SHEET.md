# QA SHEET (second pass): the 60-second summary, 30 hard questions with answers, and the 8 still open

## 0. The 60-second summary (about 170 words)
"Imagine a secure-boot check that must decide whether a key is right, and an attacker who can disturb one wire for one clock tick. I modelled that on a small circuit in three versions. The plain one and the one with two copies of the check both fail with a single disturbed wire. The one with three copies looks safe: you need three disturbances. But when I changed one assumption at a time, for instance letting the attacker disturb the final accept signal, or an input wire, or start from a different power-up state, that safety dropped to a single disturbance. Even the same design source, run through different synthesis settings, needed one, two or three. So the lesson is not that three copies are good or bad. It is that 'resists two faults' means nothing unless you say what counts as a fault and which netlist and goal you tested. I built a small tool that reruns the question under each assumption and shows which one carries the answer. It is all simulation of a toy on a laptop; published tools already do parts of this, I claim nothing new and nothing about real chips." (`research/c3/ds/DS_REPORT.md` s2.1)

## 1. The DS review script: the 10 hardest questions
**D1. Your headline V2 result flips to 1 under any one assumption change. So what did you show?** That is the result, not an objection to it. V2 costs 3 in one scope cell and 1 in the others; V2 is not separable from V1 outside that cell (B2, 360 cells). An unqualified "resists 2 faults" is not meaningful. I do not claim V2 is secure. [`research/c2/b2_scope/B2_REPORT.md`]
**D2. Isn't "depends on how you count" a truism?** As a bare sentence, yes. The content is which rules change the verdict and which do not: set/reset/branch models leave V2 at 3, hold models give no attack up to 3, correlated and input models give 1 to 2 (B1). I cannot give a physical proof that one rule is right; my headline is stem=1 because it is the field's convention (FIRMER counts per gate) and I report the others (R22). I never say "physically right".
**D3. What does your tool do that SYNFI and FIRMER do not?** Nothing I can claim as new. The repo says nothing is established as new. SYNFI measured synthesis dependence itself (3 to 2 faults) and finds minimum faults by iterating the budget; FIRMER covers multi-cycle search with per-cycle input. I ran SYNFI once: thin one-cycle agreement from a hand-chosen start state, a two-cycle chain crashed. I did not read k-FRP or FIVER, so I make no comparison against them.
**D4. Is the V2 result a hardware result?** No. Faults are modelled single-cycle bit flips of gate outputs and flop outputs in a netlist model. No chip, glitcher or physical measurement. The SPICE work is circuit-level simulation of a gate and flops, not silicon, and its report is not finished.
**D5. Why should I believe your solver?** Own simulator (about 2.1M fault sets, 0 disagreements), boolector, 13/13 witness replays, SymbiYosys BMC (40 pairs), a random cross-check of 2040 engine runs against brute force (RT), M1's 255/255 mask check against brute force, 36/36 reproduce rows (re-run at integration). Limit: all share my fault model, so they check the encoding, not the model; B1's independent checker covered only 2 of 17 models on V2.
**D6. The 6-of-32 versus 32-of-32 figure: did you measure it?** A step measured it; a second step replicated it 64 of 64 rows with its own loop (REP), using the original's list of 8 ABC invocations. Both numbers are about the counting rule on the same 32 netlists, not about physics. Say "measured by step DA, replicated by step REP, N=6, k<=3".
**D7. You said you have an unbounded proof. Show me.** One ABC-PDR PASS for one netlist, goal word, k<=2, tool-reported, no certificate, not replicated by the reviewer. Re-runs on controls show it is not vacuous (DA2). I would not lean on it, and k-FRP, which may cover this, is unread. My bounded results are T=6, k<=3 with sweeps to 20 cycles.
**D10. Is V3 better than V2, is it secure?** Not shown secure. In the finished cells where V2 costs 1, V3 costs 3 under the word goal; many cells NOT RUN; search capped at k<=3; 8-bit `boot_ok` moves the single-net problem to the consumer; flipping `rst` three times costs 3 from arbitrary power-up. Exploratory exhibit only.
**D-bonus. Why 85 gates (apt 0.33) versus 83 (conda 0.38)?** We do not know the exact gate-count cause, but we now know why V2 can fall to 2 (P01a, `docs/report/WHY_TWO_FLIPS.md`): Encoding sets the floor (idle word touches 2 of 3 copies => 2 flips); the synthesis flow decides whether the floor is reached (1 when a flow merges the copies). A build or flow difference changes the netlist, not the underlying weakness. Earlier E2 notes: E2: synthesis is deterministic; the three compare cones are disjoint in both builds; the difference is in next-state logic mapping (one MUX on conda); apt's 2-flip attack uses an AND with fanout 3 and an OR with fanout 2; `opt_merge` or flatten-before-ABC would merge the copies and give 1.

## 2. FP's 30 Socratic questions with current answers
Format: question, short truthful answer, source. "WE DO NOT KNOW" is allowed.
**A. Definitions**
1. *What is a fault in your tool?* A one-cycle XOR on the output of a gate or a flop Q (a flop-Q flip is transient on the output, it does not overwrite stored state). [`formal/FORMAL.md` s2]
2. *What is a minimum fault set?* The smallest K with a satisfying assignment (flips plus input), found by tightening the cardinality bound until UNSAT.
3. *What does "break" mean?* `boot_word == 0x96` observed while `sig != 0xC3`. NOT the flag `boot_ok`, which falls to 1 flip on V2 (CT-1, integration step re-ran).
4. *What counts as ONE fault, which rule is correct?* [flag, now answerable] One gate-output or flop-Q flip is one fault however many gates read it (stem=1): my headline, the field's convention (FIRMER per gate). The branch rule is a finer second row. Fanout-weighted is a stress test with no physical support found. I do not say any rule is "physically right". [R22]
5. *What does "redundancy helps" mean as a number?* The minimum K rises. V0 <= V1 <= V2 held in the E2 grid (not machine-checked per row). It does not mean "secure".
**B. Assumptions**
6. *Why is the input constant, real boot data changes?* E1 and B2 also ran a fresh non-key value every cycle: same V2 numbers (V2 samples `sig` once at cycle 1 then latches); it does not generalise to designs that re-sample. The spec only makes sense if the key is never presented at any cycle.
7. *Known power-up state, what if flops power up random?* [flag, now answered by B2] With an arbitrary non-accepting power-up state V2 costs 1; with arbitrary state and no reset every design costs 0. So the V2 number needs a known init. The layout has no reset.
8. *Why are inputs and clock/reset not fault sites?* A modelling choice. With input nets as fault sites V2 costs 1 on every netlist (B2, B1). Clock/reset: no fault sites; flop_hold and clock_skip_all give no attack up to 3.
9. *Are faults independent single events?* The main model says yes. B1: one event flipping a cone or neighbourhood gives V2 1 (spike) or 2; groups of 2 give 2. EL1 (SIMULATED, ngspice, not silicon): a supply dip drooped every high gate output on the rail together and only toward 0; no flop Q flipped from a dip alone; a runt clock pulse made the flop capture D.
10. *Is "attacker omniscient" realistic?* No. It is a worst case for design checking, not an attack cost.
**C. Evidence**
11. *Why trust the solver?* See D5. It checks the encoding, not the model.
12. *What does UNSAT at k=2 prove?* "On this netlist file, fault rule, goal and input rule, no set of at most 2 flips within 6 cycles reaches the goal." Nothing about cycle 7, 3 flips, another netlist or another goal.
13. *Is the unbounded proof right?* [flag] Tool-reported. DA2: not vacuous; the k=3 "NOSTATUS" is a wrapper artefact (no `yices`). Still no certificate, same flip semantics, one netlist, not replicated by the reviewer, k-FRP unread. Keep it out of the headline.
14. *Why does apt 0.33 (85 gates, 2-flip attack) differ from conda 0.38 (83 gates, none)?* [flag] The exact tool change behind the gate count is unknown; the 2-flip weakness is the encoding (0xCC xor 0x96 touches 2 of 3 copies; `rtl/v2e.v` gives 3 on 6 of 8 flows; WHY_TWO_FLIPS.md). Known (E2): deterministic synthesis, compare cones disjoint in both, difference in next-state mapping, same verdict in all other configurations.
15. *Does the attack exist in the sky130-mapped netlist?* [flag, partly answered] EL2 ran the mapped netlists with an adapter: V2 min 3 on both (generic apt 0.33 was 2). No second solver. DA2: the three comparators are separate cells in the layout.
**D. Alternatives**
16. *Why your own solver, not SYNFI/FIRMER?* They exist and cover the method. My value is a reproducible sensitivity analysis and teaching material. Nothing new.
17. *Why not simulate the electronics, what does SPICE show?* [flag, now answered] A SPICE bridge was run (EL1, ngspice, sky130 tt/ss/ff, SIMULATED, not silicon): dips are rail-correlated and 1-to-0, a dip alone never flipped a flop Q (even 0 V for 3 ns), a clock runt makes a flop capture D. It supports correlated, directional fault classes; it does not validate the verdicts. See s3.
18. *Why not a real majority voter?* Tested (M1): a per-bit 2-of-3 vote gave full word 2 (lower than 3); one shared voter gave 1 for every non-colliding mask. Voting needs a hardened voter or a consumer that does not trust one net.
19. *Would a different state encoding change results?* Probably: accept 0x96 and fail 0x69 are complements, so a flip-all event breaks every design (EL2-3). A non-complementary encoding was NOT tested.
20. *Why cap at k<=3, T=6?* Solver cost (V2 k=3 at T=20 took 475 s). ">3" or "none" is not "secure". R20: DMR S-box (2040 gates) k=2 at T=4 found in 46 s.
**E. Consequences**
21. *Is V2 a design someone should adopt?* No. A specimen.
22. *What should a designer do with the tool?* Run it on their own post-synthesis netlist, name the tool build, goal, input treatment, init and counting rule, and read the answer as a lower bound on attacker effort under the model.
23. *Does it scale to real designs?* [flag] WE DO NOT KNOW beyond what was tried: 2040 gates at T=4, k=2 in under a minute (R20); V2 k=3 at T=20 475 s on about 70 gates; only AND/OR/XOR/NOT/MUX/DFF; async reset refused. Say "teaching scale".
24. *Is 2 versus 3 flips security-relevant?* The number is not a probability or cost: it assumes any flip can be placed exactly. We have no fault-rate data.
25. *What changes the headline?* A physical argument for one counting rule; a layout-aware correlation model; goal, input, init choices (B2); a different synthesis build (E2).
**F. Meta**
27. *What is new?* Nothing established as new.
28. *Each pivot followed a finding that killed the last claim; is the surviving claim unfalsifiable?* Partly fair. It is falsifiable in one place: with the counting rule fixed, if the verdict spread across plausible synthesis recipes were absent, the claim fails for that rule; E2/REP show it present. Pre-registering the rule list before final numbers is the fix (BC row 2).
29. *Which numbers did you re-run yourself?* Say exactly what you ran. Integration step and others re-ran: CT-1 (1 flip on `boot_ok`), `pytest tests` (189 passed (2 skipped) on 2026-11-20 (re-measured by RECON6 on the unpacked bundle); older pages said 30) and `check-reproduce` 36/36 on copies, D1's earlier re-runs, RA's quickstart, REP's replication. Step-reported only: B1 matrix, E1 free-input, E2 matrix, M1, B2, SYNFI run, SPICE data. Fuzzing "0 mismatches in about 6,700 programs" and the CMSIS issues are not reproducible from the repo.
30. *What would show you the project is wrong?* An independent simulator disagreeing with a stored witness; the PDR proof failing under a second engine; the mapped netlist giving a different minimum by a second solver; a physical argument that makes the V2 number meaningless. The first two have not happened; the last two are untested.

## 5. Weak points to admit before being asked

30. *Is the structural predictor validated, and is it faster?* No. On 163 fresh cells from 76 new netlists it was exact on 150 (92%; always predicting 1 gives 62%, the sound lower bound alone 83%), 58% (18 of 31) on cells needing 2 or more flips; within one flip 95.7%, which fails the 98% bar we set before the run; it was 1.4x slower than the solver. It is a heuristic attack search with a sound lower bound; the solver is the reference. The 928/928 figure is near-duplicate cells. (VER, `research/c4/ver/VER_REPORT.md`.) LEDGER: 71/71 on a stratified sample of the same E2 family, 4/6 on fresh RTLs (wrong on voter designs V2m and V5).
31. *Does V2 survive a supply dip?* Only inside the model: Under B3's 1-to-0-only rail model V1, V2 and V5 survive within the bound (no attack up to 3 events, T=6; V0 does not under a cone event), but a 0->1 event on the three compare copies breaks V2 and V5 with one event (V1 and V0 too): survival depends on polarity, not on triplication (LEDGER 0-to-1 control, `research/c5/ledger/`; whole-design `rise_all` stays >3 for the word goal). SPICE supports 1-to-0 for supply dips; EM literature reportedly describes set-to-1 (UNVERIFIED, search-snippet level, not checked here); the domain is the netlist hierarchy, not a physical rail; physical track closed (no extraction, selectivity unproven; L2 spread layouts failed LVS 4/4 by a known filler flow bug) (`research/c5/anchor/RAIL_RECONCILIATION.md`). If one event can choose which gates fall, 3 gates (one per copy) give a 1-event attack; V1 and V2 tie in every rail-event ledger row and differ only in the stem-flip and fanout-count rows (table 'What triplication bought', `docs/report/PROJECT_REPORT.md`); triplication buys one extra gate under a selective event (2 vs 3 gates). The SPICE basis is one tiny rail. (B3, LEDGER.)
32. *Where would the three copies have to sit?* In the stored V2 layout the three comparator cells that suffice lie within a 2.95 um radius under the selective force-to-0 (supply-dip) model; under a bit flip a different sufficient triple lies within 1.67 um and there is one supply net, so a selective local event is geometrically possible; this is a measured geometry plus a simulated verdict, with no IR-drop study. A hypothetical swap raised the radius to 4.6 um; L2 then spread the copies in a real flow run: 11 to 18 um, but a die-wide selective dip still costs 1 and LVS failed in 4 of 4 spread layouts. (LAYOUT, L2.)
33. *Did you try a better design?* Yes: V4 (sparse code) 1, V5 (voter plus alert) beats V2 only on the full-word goal in the apt flow (3 vs 2), V6 (sparse sequence) was worse (1) so the prediction in the literature sketch failed. No design beats V2 on the stored netlist. (V456.)
34. *Is the tool ready for others?* Partly: the product now has consumer bits, input sites, power-up modes, scope sweeps, width sweeps and a timeout; branch counting and per-cycle input are not shipped; licence Apache-2.0 (decided 2026-11-20); Dockerfile built and Action shell steps run only locally (not on GitHub or a normal network). (PS4, PS5, review panel W1.)
35. *Is it faster to solve now?* Yes on UNSAT cells: T3 applied the sequential-counter encoding (at least 43x to 107x where the old encoding timed out; 36/36 reproduce rows and all tests pass), but some SAT cells are slower. (R27, T3.)
36. *Which of your mask numbers could still change?* The consumer-mask table (88/96/44, 68 mismatches on apt-0.33): rechecked by M1b with two engines: no cell changed (0 of 1785; research/c4/m1b/M1B_REPORT.md) for an x-init bug found by VER in the solver copy; the width curve 0,1,1,2,2,3,3,3 was also reproduced through the product engine.
