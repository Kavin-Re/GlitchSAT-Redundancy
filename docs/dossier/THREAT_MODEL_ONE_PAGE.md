# THREAT MODEL (one page), second pass
Working title TBD. License: UNDECIDED. Paths relative to `/home/user/project/`.

**System under study.** A toy secure-boot decision: an 8-bit compare of an input `sig` with key 0xC3 decides whether the boot word (the state register) becomes 0x96 (accept) or 0x69 (reject). Three designs: V0 plain, V1 two compare copies into one shared net `ok`, V2 three compare copies each driving its own result bits, no vote [`rtl/v1.v`, `rtl/v2.v`].

**Attacker (modelled).** Knows the netlist. Chooses `sig` (any value except the key) held constant for the run (E1/B2 also run a fresh non-key value each cycle: same V2 numbers). Chooses up to K faults in total; each flips one gate output or one flop Q for one clock cycle, at a cycle of its choice among T (baseline T=6, K<=3). Worst case: all chosen together, explored symbolically by a SAT solver [`scripts/glitchsat.py` docstring; `docs/ENGINE_STATUS.md` "Model"].

**Goal (the first scope choice).** `boot_word == 0x96` observed at some cycle while `sig` is not the key. NOT the accept flag `boot_ok`: one flip of that net reaches it (CT-1; lead re-ran). A consumer that decodes only part of the word is also weaker than the full-word case (M1: full word 3 / 2 on apt; 4-bit consumer typically 1 to 2; accept flag 1).

**Scope choices that move the verdict (B2, MEASURED, 360 cells).** (1) goal word vs accept flag; (2) input nets as fault sites or not (as sites V2 = 1); (3) power-up state: known, arbitrary non-accepting (V2 = 1), arbitrary with no reset (every design 0); (4) the netlist file and synthesis build/flow/options (E2: V2 = 1, 2 or 3 over 864 configurations); (5) the fault model and counting rule (B1: 17 models; REP: 6 of 32 vs 32 of 32). **Specification note (E1):** "wrong key accepted" is only well defined if the key is never presented at any cycle; otherwise every design breaks with 0 flips.

**Not modelled (NON-goals).** Voltage, clock, EM or laser physics and any map from them to a flip; timing violations and metastability; stuck-at and multi-cycle faults (B1 adds set/reset/hold/group models as modelling choices, none calibrated to a layout); faults on clock and reset lines; scan/test access; power/side-channel leakage; software or IT-layer attacks (the original supermarket attacks were of that kind) [`docs/report/PROJECT_REPORT.md` s7]. Real glitches are analog and often hit many wires; one flipped net is a chosen abstraction (R22).

**What a PASS means.** On that netlist file, in that model, for that goal and input treatment, within T cycles and at most K flips, no attack exists (solver UNSAT, or sby PASS). One PDR PASS drops the T bound for V2 spike at k<=2 for the word goal (tool-reported, unreplicated, no certificate). It does not say the Verilog is safe, nor that a physical glitch is harmless, nor that another Yosys build, ABC option or counting rule gives the same netlist or number. MEASURED example (E2): the same `rtl/v2.v` gives min 2 (apt 0.33, 85 gates), min 3 (conda 0.38, 83 gates), or 1 (any `opt_merge`). "None up to 3" is not "secure".

**What a FAIL means.** A concrete witness: nets, cycles, input. It is replayed in an iverilog bench (fault-free control stays 0x69). The witness is not unique between runs (11 of 36 rows differ); the minimal size is stable (H1).

**Counting rule (R22).** Headline: one gate-output or flop-Q flip is one fault whatever its fanout (stem = 1), the convention of FIRMER and SYNFI. Branch-finer is a second row; fanout-weighted is a stress test only; none is "physically right".

**Assumptions that can break the headline.** Constant input (reduced for V2 spike by E1, not removed); counting rule; synthesis keeps the redundancy (E2: it often does not); generic netlist = mapped netlist (EL2: V2 min 3 mapped, one adapter, no second solver); correlated physical faults (B1: a cone/neighbourhood event gives V2 = 1; EL1 SPICE (SIMULATED): one supply dip drooped every high gate output on the rail together and only toward 0; no flop Q flip from a dip alone; a clock runt is a wrong-time capture).

**Origin note.** The original supermarket / independent-monitor idea was dropped because the observed attacks were IT-layer; incident details are from earlier agent research, UNVERIFIED.

## Dual-use and disclosure (softened per SE, `research/c3/se/SE_REPORT.md` s1)
This toolkit analyses modelled bit flips in gate-level netlists to help designers decide where redundancy is needed. It outputs net names and cycle numbers, not physical parameters: no voltage, clock, EM or laser timing and no device targets. SE judged the uplift beyond published tools (SYNFI, FIRMER, CLEAVE, uArchiFI) negligible. The designs here are toy; the weakness class (copies ending in a shared decision net) is public. It was not used on any product or hardware. Do not use it to attack systems you do not own or have permission to test. Results are bounded model results, not proofs of security or of insecurity of a real device.
**Third-party designs.** Two hobby open designs (Apache-2.0 / MIT, listed in `THIRD_PARTY.md`) were run (`real_designs/REPORT.md`, `real_designs/second/REPORT.md`); R20 ran further public designs fetched without pinned commits. The only results were generic (the decision flop itself, shared compare gate, parity blind to even-weight errors). **No design-specific weakness was found in the bounds tried, and no vulnerability in a deployed product is claimed.** This is not the same as "nothing ever needs disclosure": if a real open design were shown vulnerable, do a prior-art check first and report privately to its maintainers before any public statement (for OpenTitan the research notes mention a security contact; policy text not opened: UNVERIFIED). No such finding exists today and no outward report has been made; publication or upstream contact is the owner's decision (`handoff/CONTINUATION/OPEN_DECISIONS.md` item 8). Naming a hobby project's authors beside the word "attack" is also the owner's call.
