# 60-second spoken script
About 210 words, roughly 75 to 85 seconds spoken; cut the polarity paragraph for a strict 60. Show explainer.html; click "Flip the shared wire" at "one shared wire".

"Where do the copies meet?

A lock has three guards. In our drawing (a picture, not a result), all three send their answer down one shared wire. A flip is a glitch that turns one 0 into 1, or one 1 into 0, for one clock tick. Flip that wire and, in the picture, three guards are ignored. Any device that protects a decision with several guards can be fooled if they share one wire; here is how to check.

Give each guard its own wire and the picture needs three flips. Measured by a computer check on a small design: three flips for the 8-light result word. One caveat covers every number: the separate accept flag falls to one.

In the designs we tested, all from one recipe family, also measured: rebuild one design 32 ways from one recipe, and 26 needed two flips, six needed three. How you count a flip changes the answer.

So ask a supplier: where do the copies meet, how do you count a fault, what is checked at the end?

A power dip makes outputs fall, one to zero; a push the other way breaks even the guarded designs in our model; which is realistic depends on the attack.

A small model, checked on a computer. Nothing physical."
