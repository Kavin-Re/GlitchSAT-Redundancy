## Cases that tried to break the engine and did not (survivors, all MEASURED)
- Off-by-one cycles: `tests/cases.py` T4 (toggle flop, init 0, goal 1): N=1 NO_ATTACK, N=2 ATTACK at k=0 (first reachable at cycle 1). Correct.
- Same net flipped in two cycles charged twice: T3 (`g=XOR(s,s)`, `r<=g`, two outputs `AND(r,g)`): k=1 NO_ATTACK, k=2 ATTACK with flips at cycle 5 (hand min = 2). Correct.
- Flop-state persistence through D=Q: T1 min 1, matches a real upset (CT-14).
- Input-pin / fan-out branch faults: a pin flip is dominated by flipping the gate's own output for combinational gates, and a D-pin flip equals a Q flip next cycle, so stem-only modelling is not a gap beyond CT-2 (argument plus T6; not a formal proof).
- Differential fuzz (`tests/fuzz.py`): 60 random 1 to 3 flop, 3 to 7 gate netlists, T=4, k<=2, minimum fault count from `--reduce none`, `coi` and `full` vs an independent brute-force simulator: 0 disagreements. No bug found in the reduction logic or the PbLe encoding on this sample.
- `make test` on the copy: 10 passed (38.9 s). Engine at apt Yosys 0.33 reproduces the stated V2 results (spike netlist: none at k<=2, min 3 at k=3 with three compare-internal nets at cycle 1; RTL re-synthesis: min 2).

## Which headline claims survive
- SURVIVE (as stated, with their bounds): the encoding is internally correct for its own model (CT-15 section; fuzz); V0 and V1 break with 1 flip; V1's shared net `ok` is a single point of failure; the min-3 result for the spike V2 netlist at the stated goal and model; "answer depends on the synthesized netlist and Yosys build" (CT-4 confirms it).
- SURVIVE WITH A LARGER FOOTNOTE: "V2 resists 2 faults" and the unbounded PDR statement. They hold only for the word goal (CT-1: `boot_ok` falls at k=1), only with fault-free input nets (CT-2: one flip at k=1 once input buffers exist), only from an assumed reset state that the layout lacks (CT-3), only with constant input (CT-15), only on one netlist, and PDR is unreplicated by me (CT-5).
- DO NOT SURVIVE: any reading of the layout/LVS result as supporting the V2 security claim (CT-6, CT-7; I found no contradiction at k<=2, but the repo had no evidence); the "possibly new: symbolic attacker input" line (CT-10); using k as an attacker-effort measure (CT-9); the auto-text "independent copies" for the re-synthesized case (CT-12).
- Not refuted but not established: the novelty status (R10 itself says nothing established as new); anything physical (nothing in the repo claims it).
